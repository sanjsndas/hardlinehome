Renovation Without Regret: A Room-by-Room Checklist

Guides March 1, 2025 22 min read

Renovating a home is equal parts excitement and anxiety. Whether you are updating a single room or rethinking an entire floor plan, the decisions you make early on will echo for years. This guide distils the lessons from dozens of projects into a practical, room-by-room framework.

Start with the bones

Before choosing paint colours or sofa fabrics, assess the structural and spatial fundamentals. Floor plan efficiency, window placement, ceiling height, and circulation paths dictate what is possible. A beautiful finish cannot compensate for a cramped or awkward layout. Use graph paper or a simple CAD tool to map existing conditions and test alternative arrangements.

Prioritise light and ventilation

Natural light is the single most transformative element in any renovation. Consider removing non-structural walls to borrow light from adjacent spaces, or adding skylights and clerestory windows. Ventilation — both for air quality and thermal comfort — should be planned alongside lighting. Cross-ventilation is ideal; where not possible, mechanical systems must be specified early.

Choose materials for longevity

Trend-driven materials often age poorly. Invest in surfaces that patina gracefully: solid timber, natural stone, lime plaster, and quality ceramics. These materials tolerate daily life and acquire character over time. In wet areas, prioritise slip resistance and ease of cleaning without sacrificing tactile quality.

Plan services before finishes

Electrical outlets, plumbing runs, heating, and data cabling must be finalised before walls are closed. A common renovation regret is insufficient power points or poorly placed switches. Walk through each room imagining your daily routines — where you charge devices, where you read, where you prepare food — and specify accordingly.

Leave room for the unexpected

The best homes evolve. Resist the urge to fill every corner. Allow negative space, and choose a few pieces that can move between rooms as needs change. A renovation is not a final state; it is the beginning of a new chapter in your home's life.

Common questions
How do I choose a colour palette for an open-plan space? +

Start with a neutral base for walls and large furniture, then introduce colour through textiles, art, and accessories. This allows you to change the mood without repainting. Consider the light direction: north-facing rooms benefit from warmer tones, while south-facing rooms can handle cooler hues.

What is the ideal ceiling height for a modern home? +

While 2.7 metres is comfortable, 3 metres or more creates a sense of volume and allows for dramatic lighting. In renovations, exposing existing structure can add height. If height is limited, use vertical lines and low-profile furniture to draw the eye upward.

Is engineered wood better than solid timber? +

It depends on your priorities. Engineered wood is more stable, easier to install over concrete, and often more sustainable. Solid timber can be sanded and refinished many times, offering longevity. Both can be beautiful; choose based on moisture conditions, budget, and desired patina.

How much should I budget for a kitchen renovation? +

A realistic range is 15–30% of your home's value, though this varies widely by region and specification. Prioritise cabinetry and appliances, as these are hardest to change later. Save on decorative elements that can be updated over time.
